Habit beats goals: the pedometer principle

2026 · 2 min read

I use a pedometer app. It has a daily step goal. I rarely hit it. No calamity has ever followed from missing it. And yet the app has changed how much I walk, because the habit of walking got established even though the goal keeps getting missed.

This is the pattern I've come to trust for solo work and long personal projects.

The goal is often mythical. It's aspirational, it's a bit too ambitious, and honestly it was probably picked for motivational reasons rather than empirical ones. That's fine. The goal isn't the mechanism. The habit is.

The habit is what actually accumulates. Two focused hours on a weekday, sustained over six months, produces more than a "ship by Q3" commitment that collapses in month four and never gets rebuilt.

For solo founders and personal projects, the practical version:

  • Pick a habit you can sustain. Not the maximum sustainable, the comfortably sustainable.
  • Attach a mythical goal to it, publicly if you like. The goal keeps you honest about direction.
  • Measure the habit, not the goal. Did you show up this week? That's the metric.
  • If the habit holds, the goal eventually resolves itself. If the habit collapses, no goal will save the project.

This isn't a productivity hack. It's an acknowledgment that motivation is unreliable, that structure is negotiable, and that ritual is the only thing that actually survives contact with the rest of your life.

The corollary that bothers people: you're allowed to miss the goal. You're not allowed to break the habit. Missing a target and continuing to show up is progress. Hitting a target once and then disappearing for a month is not.

I write this partly for people who set ambitious goals and then feel guilty about missing them. The guilt is misdirected. The goal wasn't the point. Showing up was.
